## 2. What they have in common
- **One mechanic, done completely.** Every winner has one idea you can describe in a sentence (walk the planet, drive the car, explode the product). Nineteen chapters with nineteen ideas is the opposite.
- **The work is big and real.** Photography and video fill the screen. Text is short and sits in the gaps.
- **The site is one surface.** Sections do not stack; the camera moves. Transitions are camera moves, exploded views, zooms from macro to micro.
- **Real 3D of the real thing.** Lando's car, Opal's webcam, Ray‑Ban's glasses, Porsche's cars. Not generic boxes.
- **The cursor does something.** A light, a lens, a character, drawing.
- **Restraint.** Every top portfolio in 2025–26 has a short bio and a lot of white space. The energy is in the motion, not the amount of content.
- **Sound, optional, synced to scroll.** Why Zero and The Boat use it; the rest stay silent.

## 4. The direction I propose: "Night build"
One continuous WebGL scene. It starts in the dark with a single marker line being drawn (his habit). The line becomes the edge of a real stand model, which assembles around it. The camera pulls back: we are in the hall at night. Scrolling walks the hall; each project is a stand that lights up as the cursor‑light reaches it, with the built photograph cutting in full screen. The years run as one track along the hall floor. At the end the doors open (hold to proceed) and it is 20:40, the photo everyone took. First person throughout. Short copy. One palette: black, warm tungsten, one accent.

This needs real assets. Without them it becomes generic boxes again, which is the thing that made v12 wear thin.

**Alternate, lighter:** "Contact sheet": no WebGL, photography and video only, the whole site a film roll you scrub; Porsche‑style handoffs between projects; kinetic type for his voice. Faster to build, works on phones, depends on footage.
